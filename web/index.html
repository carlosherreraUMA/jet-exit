Who yields the aisle?

After landing, people already standing in the aisle usually stop to let each row out. The hypothesis: it would be faster if the aisle never stopped and those still seated waited for a real gap, using the wait to get their things ready. Both planes carry exactly the same passengers; only that rule changes.

How the model works

Every number is a parameter with its source; those without data yet are estimates waiting for video measurements (see DATA.md).

  1. Continuous aisle. Each person has a body depth (28-45 cm), a free walking speed (0.5-1.1 m/s; median 0.8 m/s, measured by Schultz) and limited acceleration. They keep 15 cm from the person ahead when stopped and a 0.6 s headway when moving; once stopped, they wait for 25 cm more before starting again, so queues restart as a wave.
  2. Inside the row. Everyone gets ready seated at the same time (0-10 s). The aisle seat leaves first; the others stand up and shuffle sideways once there is room.
  3. Bags. 65% have a bag overhead. If they can reach it from their row, 80% take it down while they wait; otherwise they take it down in the aisle, blocking it. Carrying a bag, they take up 25 cm more and walk 15% slower.
  4. Bins. 28% of bags are not above their row. If the bag is ahead, they stop to pick it up on the way; if it is behind and out of reach, they wait until the aisle up to it is empty, reserve it and walk against the flow.
  1. Groups and stops. In a third of the half-rows, neighbours travel together: whoever is in the aisle steps a little ahead and waits for the others to get out behind them, whatever the rule. They give up if someone else gets in between or after 30 s. And 15% stop once on the way out (a second item, a coat) for 1.5 to 12 s.
  2. The rule being compared. When approaching a row where someone is getting up, a walker decides once whether to stop and let the whole row out (probability p_yield).
  3. Gaps. Under any rule, someone at the row edge may step out without being let in if there is room and nobody is closer than 1 m plus 1.5 s per m/s of speed.
  4. Calibration. With courtesy, a full A320 takes 13.2 min (about 14 pax/min), a little slower than observed: 15-17 pax/min, and 6:27 to 13 min with at least 80% occupancy. Groups and stops are still estimates waiting for video data.
